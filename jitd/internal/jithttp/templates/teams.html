Connect Microsoft Teams

  1. Register the app in Entra. In the Microsoft Entra admin centre, go to App registrations → New registration, name it JIT access and pick Accounts in this organizational directory only. Copy the Application (client) ID and the Directory (tenant) ID. Then under Certificates & secrets → Client secrets → New client secret, copy the secret's Value (not its ID; the value is shown once).
  2. Create the bot and turn on socket mode. In the Teams Developer Portal, under Tools → Bot management, create a bot using the app ID above. Open it, choose Configure, and under Delivery method pick Socket mode. Socket mode is only available for newly created bots.
  3. Fill these in.
  4. Install the app in a team. Once connected, download the app package below and upload it in Teams (Apps → Manage your apps → Upload a custom app), then add it to a team. Uploading custom apps has to be allowed in the Teams admin centre.

The client secret is kept in jitd's database and is never shown again, here or in the API. To keep it out of the database, put TEAMS_APP_ID, TEAMS_APP_PASSWORD and TEAMS_TENANT_ID (and TEAMS_ALLOWED_DOMAINS) in {{$st.EnvFile}} instead and restart jitd.

How it works

  • Each approval flow can post new requests to a channel, and send each approver a direct message. The card updates itself as the request is approved, declined, expires or is revoked.
  • Approve and Decline in Teams run the same checks as the browser: who may approve, no self-approval unless the flow allows it, how many approvals it needs. Revoking stays in the browser. Decline asks for a reason in the card itself, and only the person who pressed it sees that form.
  • In the browser, Tailscale says who you are. In Teams, jitd goes by your work address: you must be a member of the connected tenant (not a guest), your address must be in an allowed domain and must be your Tailscale login, and that login must own a device on the tailnet. Decisions made this way are marked via Teams on the request, with the account that made them.
  • For a flow that should only be decided in the browser, turn on Browser only on its page: its cards then carry no buttons.
  • Message the bot (or @mention it in a channel) with status to see what is waiting for you and your own requests, request to ask for access, or help for the list. /jit in front is optional.
  • Direct messages only reach people the app can see. Teams has no way to look somebody up by address, so jitd reads the rosters of the teams the app is installed in. An approver in none of them is listed with a ⚠ and gets no direct message; the channel post and the browser still reach them.
  • Socket mode is in public preview and only available for newly created bots. Microsoft recommends an HTTPS endpoint for production. jitd uses socket mode because it is what keeps jitd off the public internet.
